# ESAT exam guide and FAQs

## 1. Exam overview

### 1.1 Key facts

| Item | Details |
|------|------|
| Full name | Engineering and Science Admissions Test |
| Organiser | UAT-UK (University Admissions Tests – UK), a non-profit organisation jointly established by Imperial College London and the University of Cambridge in 2023. The test is delivered by Pearson VUE. |
| Format | Computer-based test at a Pearson VUE test centre |
| Duration | Three modules of 40 minutes each; 120 minutes in total |
| Questions | 27 multiple-choice questions per module; 81 in total |
| Scoring | Each module is scored separately on a 1.0–9.0 scale using a Rasch item response theory model; no negative marking |
| First sitting | October 2024 for 2025 entry, replacing ENGAA and NSAA |
| Fees | £78 at UK centres / £133 at international centres, covering three modules; eligible candidates may receive a bursary |
| Entry year | For entry the following autumn: for example, the October 2026 sitting is for 2027 entry, unlike STEP |

**Key distinction:** ESAT is taken in October of the year before entry, for example October 2026 for 2027 entry, and supports selection during the application process. STEP is taken in June of the year of entry, for example June 2026 for 2026 entry, and forms part of a conditional offer. Their timing and roles in admissions differ.

### 1.2 What the test assesses

ESAT assesses the following skills in applicants to engineering and natural sciences degrees at leading UK universities:

- **Mathematical reasoning:** deep understanding and flexible use of mathematical concepts based on A-Level content.
- **Problem solving:** analysing unfamiliar problems and transferring knowledge to new contexts rather than relying on memorisation.
- **Accuracy under time pressure:** making quick decisions and calculating efficiently to answer 27 questions in 40 minutes per module.
- **Comparable performance across backgrounds:** a standardised assessment that supports fair comparison between applicants from different education systems.

### 1.3 Universities and courses

The following summary is based on UAT-UK information. Requirements may change by university, course and year. Always check the latest official admissions requirements when applying.

| University | Courses | Modules | Notes |
|------|------|------|------|
| Cambridge | Engineering | Mathematics 1 + Mathematics 2 + Physics | All applicants must take ESAT; results inform interview selection and admissions decisions. |
| Cambridge | Natural Sciences | Mathematics 1 + Mathematics 2 + one of Physics, Chemistry or Biology | The third module depends on the intended subject area: Physics, Chemistry or Biology. |
| Cambridge | Chemical Engineering and Biotechnology | Mathematics 1 + Mathematics 2 + Chemistry | Chemistry is normally the third module; some applicants may choose Biology. |
| Cambridge | Veterinary Medicine | Mathematics 1 + Biology + Chemistry | Biology and Chemistry are required. |
| Oxford | Engineering Science, Physics, Chemistry and related courses | Course-specific; check Oxford's latest requirements | Oxford joins ESAT from the 2026 testing cycle for 2027 entry. |
| Imperial College London | Engineering courses, Physics, Chemistry and related courses | Mathematics 1 + Mathematics 2 + Physics or Chemistry | Most engineering and science courses require ESAT results. |
| UCL | Selected engineering and science courses | Course-specific | Some courses consider ESAT in admissions. |

**Please note:**

1. Module requirements vary between universities and between courses at the same university.
2. Check your target course's official admissions page for the relevant year.
3. Some universities may accept ESAT and other tests, such as TMUA, as alternatives.

### 1.4 Test structure

Each module lasts **40 minutes** and contains **27 multiple-choice questions**.

| Module | Content | Typical applicants |
|------|------|------|
| Mathematics 1 | Core A-Level Mathematics: algebra, functions, coordinate geometry, sequences, trigonometry, exponentials and logarithms | Compulsory for all ESAT candidates, including all Cambridge courses using ESAT |
| Mathematics 2 | More advanced standard A-Level Mathematics, including calculus and vectors; excludes Further Mathematics topics such as complex numbers and differential equations | Required for courses such as Cambridge Engineering and Natural Sciences |
| Physics | A-Level Physics: mechanics, electromagnetism, thermal physics, waves and atomic physics | Engineering and physics |
| Chemistry | A-Level Chemistry: atomic structure, bonding, thermodynamics, kinetics and organic chemistry | Chemistry, chemical engineering and chemistry-focused natural sciences |
| Biology | A-Level Biology: cell biology, genetics, physiology and ecology | Biology, biological natural sciences and veterinary medicine |

Candidates take **three modules**, giving **120 minutes and 81 questions** in total.

**Note:** Consult the official ESAT Content Specification for the detailed syllabus. Mathematics 1 and Mathematics 2 are based on standard A-Level Mathematics and overlap in places. Mathematics 2 includes more advanced topics, such as calculus. Neither includes A-Level Further Mathematics content.

|------|------|------|
| UCAS deadline | 15 October 2026 | 15 October 2026 |

**Key points:** October is the main sitting and is compulsory for Cambridge and Oxford applicants. January is available only to candidates whose target universities accept it and who have not already taken ESAT in October. You cannot take both sittings and submit the better score. Confirm your target university's accepted sitting before booking.

## 2. Frequently asked questions

### Registration

**Q1: How do I register for ESAT?**

Register online. First create a free candidate account at esat-tmua.ac.uk; account creation for 2027 entry opens on 1 June 2026. Once booking opens on 20 July 2026, sign in, choose Add Test and the required modules, select a Pearson VUE centre and date, and pay. Registration does not need to be arranged through your school. Follow the latest official instructions.

**Q2: When is the registration deadline?**

For 2027 entry, booking closes at **18:00 BST on 28 September 2026** for October and **18:00 GMT on 21 December 2026** for January, according to UAT-UK's Key Dates page. Late bookings are not accepted. Book early to secure a place, and check the official dates for your cycle.

**Q3: Which test centres can I use?**

ESAT is delivered at Pearson VUE authorised centres worldwide, including the UK, mainland China, Hong Kong and Macao. Search by location in the booking system. Choose a convenient centre early, as popular locations can fill up.

**Q4: Can I register for both October and January?**

No. **Only one sitting is permitted per application cycle**, either October or January. **Cambridge and Oxford applicants must take October.** January is available only if your target university accepts it. Confirm the accepted sitting before booking.

**Q5: Must I submit my UCAS application before registering?**

No. ESAT registration and UCAS applications are separate, so registration can take place before or after your UCAS submission deadline. The UCAS deadline, usually 15 October, is close to the October test window, so plan both in advance. Provide your UCAS Personal ID when registering so results can be matched to your application.

### Choosing modules

**Q6: Which modules are available, and must I take three?**

There are five modules: Mathematics 1, Mathematics 2, Physics, Chemistry and Biology. You must take three, according to your target university and course requirements. Cambridge Engineering normally requires Mathematics 1, Mathematics 2 and Physics. For Natural Sciences, the third module depends on your intended subject area: physics, chemistry or biology.

**Q7: How do I choose my modules?**

Use the official requirements for your target courses:

1. Check each course's admissions page for the required combination.
2. If applying to several courses or universities, consider their requirements together so all are covered.
3. Select the appropriate modules when booking through Pearson VUE.

Common combinations are Mathematics 1 + Mathematics 2 + Physics for engineering; Mathematics 1 + Mathematics 2 + Chemistry for chemistry; and Mathematics 1 + Biology + Chemistry for biology or veterinary medicine.

**Q8: Can I change modules after booking?**

Changes can usually be made in the Pearson VUE system before the booking deadline. They are generally not permitted after it. Check the official and centre-specific change policies.

**Q9: How do Mathematics 1 and Mathematics 2 differ?**

- **Mathematics 1:** core pure mathematics, including algebra, functions, coordinate geometry, sequences, trigonometry, exponentials and logarithms.
- **Mathematics 2:** more advanced pure mathematics, including differentiation, integration and vectors, within standard A-Level Mathematics. Further Mathematics topics such as complex numbers and differential equations are outside the ESAT scope.

The modules overlap, but Mathematics 2 is broader and more demanding. Cambridge Engineering and Natural Sciences normally require both.

### Preparation

**Q10: How difficult is ESAT?**

This guide places ESAT above standard A-Level examinations but below STEP and extended-response tests such as STEP/MAT, with a level broadly comparable to the former ENGAA/NSAA. Questions require deep understanding and flexible use of core concepts rather than routine substitution into formulae. Fast decisions and calculation under time pressure are major challenges.

**Q11: Are official preparation resources available?**

Resources at esat-tmua.ac.uk/prepare include:

- **ESAT Guide:** format, question types and scoring.
- **Specimen Papers:** sample questions and answers showing the test style.
- **Practice Papers:** complete papers for timed practice.
- **Past Papers:** previous tests, released as more sittings become available.
- **Mathematics Notes:** official mathematics revision notes.
- **Logic and Proof Notes:** supplementary material for mathematical reasoning.

Use official resources as the core of your preparation, alongside A-Level textbooks for topic revision.

**Q12: Can I use a calculator?**

No. **Neither personal nor on-screen calculators are allowed at any point in ESAT.** Dictionaries are also prohibited. The official wording quoted in this guide is: "You will not be able to use a calculator or dictionary." All calculations must be done by hand, and the numbers are generally designed for this.

**Q13: How does ESAT relate to STEP and TMUA?**

| Test | Universities using it | Format | Indicative difficulty |
|------|------|------|------|
| STEP | Mainly Cambridge; Warwick and Imperial in some circumstances | Extended written solutions | Highest; approaching or reaching competition level |
| ESAT | Cambridge, Oxford, Imperial, UCL and others | Multiple choice | Above A-Level, below competition level |
| TMUA | Cambridge for selected courses; Oxford, Imperial, LSE, Warwick and others | Multiple choice | Above A-Level, below STEP |

These are separate tests and are not generally interchangeable. Some universities, such as Warwick, may accept more than one test as alternative conditions. ESAT and TMUA share the UAT-UK organiser but assess different subjects for different courses.

**Q14: How long should I prepare?**

Allow around **3–6 months** of structured preparation:

- **First 1–2 months:** review each topic in the ESAT Content Specification and address gaps in the relevant A-Level content.
- **Next 1–2 months:** work through specimen and practice papers to become familiar with the questions and pace.
- **Final 4–6 weeks:** complete timed mocks at 40 minutes per module and refine time allocation.

Practise quick decisions and eliminating incorrect options while reducing careless errors.

### Test-day format

**Q15: Is there a break between modules?**

The three modules run consecutively on the same day, with short intervals, usually around 5–10 minutes, for system transitions and preparation. Each module is 40 continuous minutes without an internal break. Allow around 2.5–3 hours including intervals, and follow your Pearson VUE centre's schedule.

**Q16: What does the test interface look like?**

You take ESAT on a computer at a Pearson VUE centre and select answers with the mouse. The interface provides question navigation, flags for questions to revisit and a timer. No calculator is provided. Use the erasable board or writing materials supplied by the centre for working.

**Q17: Can I skip questions and return to them later?**

Yes. Use question navigation to skip difficult questions and return if time allows. Answer confident questions first. There is no negative marking, so attempt every question, even if you need to guess.

**Q18: Is rough paper provided?**

Centres usually provide an erasable board or rough paper. Arrangements may vary, so follow your Pearson VUE centre's instructions. Use it for working, and select your final answers in the computer interface.

**Q19: What may I bring?**

- **Bring:** valid identification, such as a passport or identity card, matching your registration details.
- **Do not bring into the test:** calculators, phones, smartwatches, other electronic devices, reference books, notes or food. Some centres allow a transparent water bottle.
- **Provided by the centre:** a computer, rough paper or a board, and a pen.

Check the latest official and centre-specific instructions.

### Results

**Q20: How are ESAT results reported?**

Each module is scored separately on a **1.0–9.0 scale**, to one decimal place. Scoring uses a Rasch item response theory model, with a median around 4.5 and the 90th percentile around 7.0. **Each module is equated and scaled separately; no total or average score is provided.** Incorrect answers do not lose marks. Results appear in your UAT-UK account and are sent automatically to your chosen universities.

**Q21: What is a good score?**

The following are indicative benchmarks:

- **7.0+:** excellent, around the top 10%, and competitive.
- **5.0–7.0:** good, roughly the top 30%, described as a common range for offers at leading universities.
- **Around 4.5:** median performance.
- **Below 4.0:** below average and less competitive.

Successful Cambridge Engineering and Natural Sciences applicants often score around 5.0–7.0 or higher in each module. These are reference points, not fixed thresholds; requirements vary by year, course and college.

**Q22: When are results released?**

For 2027 entry, October results are released on **16 November 2026**, and January results on **8 February 2027**, according to UAT-UK's Key Dates page. View them in your UAT-UK account; they are also sent to your chosen universities.

**Q23: Can I retake the test if my score is disappointing?**

You may take **only one sitting per application cycle**, so you cannot retake in the same cycle. Cambridge or Oxford applicants who sit in October cannot retake in January. A further attempt would usually be in October of the following year, with a gap year, and would count only for the new application cycle. Results from different years are separate and are not combined.

**Q24: Can I cancel or withhold my score?**

No. Results are automatically sent to the universities you apply to that use ESAT. There is no score cancellation or Score Choice option. As only one sitting is permitted per cycle, you cannot choose the best of several scores.

### Access and support

**Q25: What should candidates in mainland China consider?**

- Check the location and availability of authorised centres; places may be limited.
- Ensure your passport or other identification remains valid on test day.
- Prepare to read the test in English.
- Contact your centre early, as registration arrangements may differ from those at UK schools.
- For security reasons, candidates in mainland China, Hong Kong and Macao have restricted dates for 2027 entry: **12 or 13 October 2026**, or **6 January 2027**. No exceptions are offered.
- Follow the latest official registration instructions.

**Q26: How do candidates with special educational needs apply for access arrangements?**

Apply through your UAT-UK account and provide supporting evidence. Arrangements may include extra time, a separate room, a reader or scribe, larger text or assistive equipment. Review can take up to **10 working days**, and approval is required before booking.

For 2027 entry, application deadlines are **14 September 2026 at 18:00 BST** for October and **7 December 2026 at 18:00 GMT** for January. Applying by these dates allows time to book after approval. Check the official policy for the available arrangements and process.

**Q27: Is financial support available?**

UAT-UK offers a bursary covering the full test fee for **eligible UK candidates in financial need**. Apply through your UAT-UK account **before booking and paying**; fees already paid are not refunded under this scheme. Review can take up to **5 working days**.

For 2027 entry, deadlines are **21 September 2026 at 18:00 BST** for October and **14 December 2026 at 18:00 GMT** for January. See the official bursary page for eligibility.
