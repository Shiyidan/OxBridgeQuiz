# STEP exam guide and FAQs

## 1. Exam overview

### 1.1 Key facts

| Item | Details |
|------|------|
| Full name | Sixth Term Examination Paper |
| Organiser | **OCR (Oxford, Cambridge and RSA Examinations) since 2024**, taking over from Cambridge Assessment Admissions Testing (CAAT). The University of Cambridge Department of Mathematics is closely involved in setting the papers. |
| Format | Paper-based, with handwritten answers |
| Duration | 3 hours per paper |
| Questions | Extended-response problems with several parts; full working and proofs are required |
| Scoring | 20 marks per question; answer six for a maximum of 120 per paper. Grades: S / 1 / 2 / 3 / U. |
| First sitting | STEP dates back to around 1987. The current STEP 2 and STEP 3 format continues following the discontinuation of STEP 1 after 2020; STEP 1 has not been offered since 2021. |
| Fees | Standard OCR entry fee: **£102.50 per paper in the UK / £142.25 overseas**. Centres may charge an additional administration fee; check locally. |
| Entry year | For entry in the same autumn: for example, June 2025 for October 2025 entry, unlike ESAT |

**Key distinction:** ESAT is taken in October of the year before entry, for example October 2025 for 2026 entry. STEP is taken in June of the year of entry, for example June 2025 for 2025 entry, and forms part of a conditional offer.

### 1.2 What the test assesses

STEP assesses the following skills in applicants to mathematics degrees at leading UK universities:

- **Mathematical reasoning:** deep understanding and flexible use of concepts based on A-Level content.
- **Problem solving:** modelling and working through unfamiliar mathematical problems.
- **Sustained reasoning and proof:** constructing multi-step, layered mathematical arguments.
- **Concentration and accuracy under time pressure:** completing six demanding problems in three hours.

| University | Courses | Papers or tests | Typical conditions (indicative) |
|------|------|------|------|

| Cambridge | Mathematics with Physics | STEP II + STEP III | As above |

- Oxford Mathematics previously used MAT. TMUA replaces it from the 2026 testing cycle for 2027 entry. Oxford does not use STEP.
- Some universities use STEP as an offer condition; others use it as supporting evidence or an alternative.
- **Always check the official admissions requirements for your intended year of entry.**

### 1.4 Test structure

| Feature | STEP 2 | STEP 3 |
|------|------|------|
| Content | A-Level Mathematics + AS Further Mathematics | A-Level Further Mathematics |
| Duration | 3 hours | 3 hours |
| Questions offered | 12: eight pure mathematics, two mechanics, two probability and statistics | 12: eight pure mathematics, two mechanics, two probability and statistics |
| Questions to answer | Six; if you answer more, your six highest-scoring answers count | Six; if you answer more, your six highest-scoring answers count |
| Marks | 20 per question, maximum 120 | 20 per question, maximum 120 |

There are up to two papers, held in separate sessions on the same day or nearby dates.

**Note:** STEP 1 ended after 2020 and has not been offered since 2021. Only STEP 2 and STEP 3 remain. OCR and Cambridge's mathematics department specify 12 questions per paper: eight pure, two mechanics and two probability and statistics.

**Key points:** STEP is held once a year, in June. As results arrive in August, universities make conditional offers and confirm whether the conditions have been met after results are released. **There is no same-year resit if you miss the required grades; a retake requires the following year and usually a gap year.**

## 2. Frequently asked questions

### Registration

**Q1: How do I register for STEP?**

STEP is run by OCR, and registration is through an authorised test centre. Many UK schools and colleges teaching A-Level Mathematics are authorised centres, so candidates usually register through their school. Private candidates must contact an authorised centre themselves. Follow the latest OCR registration instructions.

**Q2: When is the registration deadline?**

Usually early May, around 5–6 weeks before the test. Register early to secure a place. Exact dates vary each year, so check the official announcement.

**Q3: Which test centres can I use?**

Authorised centres include selected schools and colleges in the UK and overseas, including some centres in mainland China. Consult the official test website for the centre list. Contact a centre early to confirm whether it accepts external candidates.

**Q4: Can I register for both STEP II and STEP III?**

Yes. Most Cambridge Mathematics applicants need both papers. They are taken in separate sessions, usually on the same day or nearby dates. You can register for both together.

**Q5: Do I have to register through my school?**

Registration is usually through a school or college. If your school is not authorised, or you are a private candidate, contact an authorised centre directly. STEP registration is separate from UCAS. OCR releases results, which are available to candidates and the universities they apply to.

### Choosing papers

**Q6: How do STEP II and STEP III differ?**

- **STEP II:** based on A-Level Mathematics and AS Further Mathematics. It is more demanding than A-Level but does not require the full Further Mathematics syllabus.
- **STEP III:** based on the full A-Level Further Mathematics syllabus, requiring broader advanced knowledge and presenting the greatest challenge.

Both last three hours and require six answers, but STEP III covers a wider range of material and more demanding problems.

**Q7: Which STEP papers should I take?**

Follow your target university and course requirements. Cambridge Mathematics normally requires both STEP II and STEP III. Other universities may require only one. Check the official requirements for your application year.

**Q8: Can I change papers after registering?**

Changes are usually possible before the registration deadline but generally not afterwards. Follow the official and centre-specific policies.

**Q9: Can I take just one STEP paper?**

Yes, if that meets your target university's requirements. Some universities need only one result. Cambridge Mathematics requires specified grades in both STEP II and STEP III.

### Preparation

**Q10: How difficult is STEP?**

STEP is one of the UK's most demanding undergraduate mathematics admissions tests, substantially above A-Level Mathematics and Further Mathematics. It emphasises understanding, problem solving and sustained reasoning rather than routine use of formulae. Its focus differs from competitions such as BMO: STEP places greater emphasis on academic mathematical reasoning rather than competition techniques.

**Q11: Is there an official textbook or reading list?**

There is no prescribed textbook. Official resources include:

- **STEP Specification:** the topics that may be assessed.
- **Past papers and mark schemes:** the main preparation resource, covering many years.
- **STEP Support Programme:** a free Cambridge online course at https://maths.org/step/, with topic-based exercises and explanations; strongly recommended.

Use A-Level Mathematics and Further Mathematics textbooks alongside past-paper practice.

**Q12: Can I use a calculator?**

**No.** Calculators are prohibited throughout STEP. **No formula booklet is provided:** formula sheets were removed in the 2019 reforms, so you need to know the formulae in the specification. Any required formula beyond the specification will be supplied in the question. All calculations are by hand, and the numbers are generally designed for this.

**Q13: How does STEP relate to MAT and TMUA?**

| Test | Universities using it | Format | Indicative difficulty |
|------|------|------|------|
| STEP | Mainly Cambridge; Warwick and Imperial in some circumstances | Extended written solutions | Highest; approaching or reaching competition level |
| MAT (discontinued) | Oxford Mathematics and Computer Science move to TMUA from the 2026 testing cycle for 2027 entry. Imperial has also stopped using MAT. The final MAT sitting was in October 2025. | A mixture of multiple-choice and extended-response questions | Medium to high |
| TMUA | Imperial, Cambridge, Durham, LSE, Oxford, Warwick and UCL: seven universities | Multiple choice | Above A-Level, below STEP |

These are separate tests and are not generally interchangeable. Some universities, such as Warwick, may accept different tests as alternative conditions.

**Q14: How long should I prepare?**

Allow **at least six months** of structured preparation:

- **First 2–3 months:** review the STEP Specification, address gaps in A-Level and Further Mathematics, and work through the STEP Support Programme.
- **Next 2–3 months:** practise past questions by topic to become familiar with their style.
- **Final 4–6 weeks:** complete timed mocks at three hours per paper and refine time allocation and question selection.

### Test-day format

**Q15: Is there a break between STEP II and STEP III?**

The papers are usually held on different dates, or in morning and afternoon sessions on the same day, so there is an interval between them. Each paper itself is three continuous hours without a break.

**Q16: What does the paper look like?**

Questions are printed, and answers are handwritten in a separate answer booklet. Show full working and proofs; a final answer alone does not earn marks. Mark schemes award method marks, so partially correct reasoning can earn partial credit.

**Q17: Can I choose which questions to answer?**

Yes. Choose any six questions without a quota for pure mathematics, mechanics or statistics. If you answer more than six, your six highest-scoring answers count. Choose according to your strengths; many candidates focus on pure mathematics.

**Q18: Is rough paper provided?**

Use the question paper or blank space in the answer booklet for rough working. Centres do not normally provide separate rough paper. Write all working that you want marked in the answer booklet.

**Q19: What may I bring?**

- **Bring:** valid identification and basic stationery, such as pens, pencils, a ruler and compasses.
- **Do not bring into the test:** calculators, phones, smartwatches, other electronic devices, reference books, notes or bilingual dictionaries, explicitly prohibited from 2023.
- **Provided by the centre:** question papers and answer booklets. **No formula booklet is provided**; formulae beyond the specification are supplied in the question when needed.

Follow the latest official and centre-specific instructions.

### Results

**Q20: How are STEP results reported?**

Each paper receives a separate grade:

| Grade | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **S** | Outstanding; the highest grade |
| **1** | Very Good |
| **2** | Good |
| **3** | Satisfactory |
| **U** | Unclassified |

Results include both the grade and a raw mark out of 120. Grade boundaries change each year according to paper difficulty and candidate performance.

**Q21: What is a good result?**

- **Grade 1 or above** is generally considered competitive.
- A typical Cambridge Mathematics offer requires **Grade 1 in both STEP II and STEP III**, often written as 1,1.
- Some colleges may require **S,1** or **S,S**.
- Other universities may ask for Grade 2 or Grade 3.

Boundaries vary. As historical reference points, rather than fixed official thresholds, Grade 1 often requires around **60–75 out of 120**, and Grade S around **80–95 or more out of 120**, depending on the year and paper.

**Q22: When are results released?**

Usually in **mid-August**, close to or on A-Level results day. Results are available through your test centre and are also sent to UCAS and your chosen universities.

**Q23: Can I retake STEP if my result is disappointing?**

STEP is held only once a year, in June. If you miss the conditions of your offer:

- There is no same-year resit, and your place may not be confirmed.
- You would need to retake the following June, usually with a gap year; the result counts only for the new application cycle.
- Results from different years are separate and are not combined.

**Q24: Can I cancel or withhold my result?**

Results are automatically sent to UCAS and your chosen universities. **Check the latest official policy** to establish whether cancellation or a Score Choice option is available.

### Access and support

**Q25: What should candidates in mainland China consider?**

- Check the location and availability of authorised centres; places may be limited.
- Ensure your passport or other identification remains valid on test day.
- Prepare to read and write mathematics in English.
- Contact the centre early, as registration arrangements may differ from those at UK schools.
- If registering through your school, confirm that it is an authorised centre.
- **Follow the latest official registration instructions.**

**Q26: How do candidates with special educational needs apply for access arrangements?**

Apply through your test centre with supporting evidence. Arrangements may include extra time, a separate room, larger text or assistive equipment. **Apply well before the registration deadline**, preferably at least six weeks in advance. Follow the official policy for the available arrangements and process.

**Q27: Is financial support available?**

**Check the latest official announcements.** Some centres or organisations may offer limited assistance for financial hardship. Ask your school or the organiser about the current arrangements.

## 4. STEP / ESAT / TMUA at a glance

| Feature | STEP | ESAT | TMUA |
|------|------|------|------|
| Full name | Sixth Term Examination Paper | Engineering and Science Admissions Test | Test of Mathematics for University Admission |
| Organiser | OCR since 2024; formerly CAAT | UAT-UK, founded by Imperial and Cambridge | UAT-UK, founded by Imperial and Cambridge |
| Subject areas | Mainly mathematics | Engineering and natural sciences | Mathematics, computer science, economics and related subjects |
| Main universities | Cambridge, Warwick and Imperial in some circumstances | Cambridge, Oxford, Imperial and UCL | Imperial, Cambridge, Durham, LSE, Oxford, Warwick and UCL: seven universities |
| Question format | Extended written solutions | Multiple choice | Multiple choice |
| Number of questions | Answer six questions per paper | 27 per module × 3 modules = 81 | 20 per paper × 2 papers = 40 |
| Duration | 3 hours per paper | 40 minutes per module × 3 = 120 minutes | 75 minutes per paper × 2 = 150 minutes |
| Delivery | Paper-based | Computer-based at Pearson VUE | Computer-based at Pearson VUE |
| Test timing | June in the year of entry | Mainly October in the year before entry | Mainly October in the year before entry |
| Results | August in the year of entry | Late November in the preceding year | Late November in the preceding year |
| Scoring | Grades S / 1 / 2 / 3 / U plus a raw mark | 1.0–9.0 scale using an IRT model | 1.0–9.0 scale using an IRT model |
| Role in admissions | A condition of an offer | Selection during the application process | Selection during the application process |
| Retakes | The following June, usually requiring a gap year | One sitting per cycle; a retake requires the following October and usually a gap year | One sitting per cycle; a retake requires the following October and usually a gap year |
| Calculators | Not allowed; no formula booklet | Not allowed or provided | Not allowed or provided |
| Fees | £102.50 per paper in the UK / £142.25 overseas | £78 in the UK / £133 internationally | £78 in the UK / £133 internationally |
| Indicative difficulty | Highest; approaching competition level | Above A-Level, below competition level | Above A-Level, below STEP |
| Preparation resources | STEP Support Programme and past papers | ESAT Guide, specimen papers, practice papers and past papers | TMUA Guide, specimen papers, practice papers and past papers |

**Important:** This guide is based on historical information and provides a general reference. Policies may change each year. **Always check the latest official announcements for dates, fees and detailed rules.** This is not an official statement.
