# TMUA exam guide and FAQs

## 1. Exam overview

### 1.1 Key facts

| Item | Details |
|------|------|
| Full name | Test of Mathematics for University Admission |
| Organiser | UAT-UK (University Admissions Tests – UK), a non-profit organisation jointly established by Imperial College London and the University of Cambridge in 2023. The test is delivered by Pearson VUE. |
| Format | Computer-based test at a Pearson VUE test centre |
| Duration | Two papers of 75 minutes each; 150 minutes in total |
| Questions | 20 multiple-choice questions per paper; 40 in total |
| Scoring | One combined score on a 1.0–9.0 scale, using a Rasch item response theory model; no negative marking |
| First sitting | Introduced in 2016 under CAAT; moved to UAT-UK in October 2024 for 2025 entry |
| Fees | £78 at UK centres / £133 at international centres, covering both papers; eligible candidates may receive a bursary |
| Entry year | For entry the following autumn: for example, the October 2026 sitting is for 2027 entry, unlike STEP |

**Key distinction:** TMUA and ESAT are both UAT-UK tests taken in October of the year before entry, but assess different subjects. TMUA focuses on mathematics, including mathematical reasoning, for courses such as mathematics, computer science and economics. ESAT covers mathematics, physics, chemistry and biology for engineering and natural sciences. STEP is taken in June of the year of entry and forms part of conditional offers for mathematics courses.

### 1.2 What the test assesses

TMUA assesses the following skills in applicants to mathematics and related degrees:

- **Applying mathematical knowledge:** flexible use of pure mathematics at Higher GCSE / AS level in unfamiliar problems, without mechanics or statistics.
- **Mathematical reasoning:** Paper 2 focuses on arguments and logic, including identifying errors in proofs, constructing counterexamples and assessing whether statements are true.
- **Accurate reading and interpretation:** extracting mathematical information from sometimes lengthy descriptions and turning it into a problem that can be solved.
- **Working efficiently:** answering 20 questions in 75 minutes requires quick decisions and efficient calculation, with about 3.75 minutes available per question.

### 1.4 Test structure

| Paper | Content and focus | Duration | Questions |
|------|------|------|------|
| Paper 1: Applications of Mathematical Knowledge | Higher GCSE / AS-level pure mathematics: algebra, functions, coordinate geometry, sequences, trigonometry, exponentials, logarithms and calculus. Questions often involve detailed descriptions, mathematical modelling and applying knowledge in new contexts. No mechanics or statistics. | 75 minutes | 20 multiple-choice questions |
| Paper 2: Mathematical Reasoning | The same pure mathematics content, plus reasoning and proof: identifying errors, constructing counterexamples, evaluating statements and understanding logical implication. It requires rigorous analysis rather than knowledge beyond the specification. No mechanics or statistics. | 75 minutes | 20 multiple-choice questions |
| Total | Both papers | 150 minutes | 40 questions |

**Note:** Consult the official TMUA Content Specification for the detailed syllabus. Both papers use the same Higher GCSE / AS-level pure mathematics content, excluding mechanics and statistics. Paper 2 additionally assesses reasoning and proof, and has a noticeably different question style.

**Key points:** TMUA and ESAT share a test window but are separate tests, with separate bookings and fees. For each test, you may sit **either October or January, not both**. Cambridge and Oxford applicants must take October. January is available only where the target university accepts that sitting. There is no option to take both and submit the higher score.

## 2. Frequently asked questions

### Registration

**Q1: How do I register for TMUA?**

Registration is online, following the same process as ESAT. First create a free candidate account at esat-tmua.ac.uk; account creation for 2027 entry opens on 1 June 2026. Once booking opens on 20 July 2026, sign in, choose Add Test, select a Pearson VUE test centre and date, and pay. You do not choose individual papers: both are compulsory. Follow the latest official registration instructions.

**Q2: When is the registration deadline?**

For 2027 entry, booking closes at **18:00 BST on 28 September 2026** for October and **18:00 GMT on 21 December 2026** for January, according to UAT-UK's Key Dates page. Late bookings are not accepted. Book early to secure a place, and check the official dates for your application cycle.

**Q3: Which test centres can I use?**

TMUA uses the same worldwide Pearson VUE authorised centre network as ESAT, including centres in the UK, mainland China, Hong Kong and Macao. Search by location in the Pearson VUE booking system. Choose a convenient centre early, as popular locations can fill up.

**Q4: Can I register for both October and January?**

No. **Only one sitting is permitted per application cycle**, either October or January. **Cambridge and Oxford applicants must take October.** January is available only if your target university accepts it. Confirm the accepted sitting before booking.

**Q5: Must I submit my UCAS application before registering?**

No. TMUA registration and UCAS applications are separate, so registration can take place before or after your UCAS submission deadline. The UCAS deadline, usually 15 October, is close to the October test window, so plan both in advance. Provide your UCAS Personal ID when registering so results can be matched to your application.

### Test papers

**Q6: How do Paper 1 and Paper 2 differ?**

Both cover Higher GCSE / AS-level pure mathematics, excluding mechanics and statistics.

- **Paper 1, Applications of Mathematical Knowledge:** applying mathematics to unfamiliar contexts, often using longer written descriptions.
- **Paper 2, Mathematical Reasoning:** analysing arguments, identifying errors in proofs, constructing counterexamples and deciding whether statements are true.

Paper 2 requires more rigorous logical analysis, rather than knowledge beyond the specification. The papers are comparable in difficulty but differ in style.

**Q7: Must I take both papers?**

Yes. TMUA reports a combined score, and completing only one paper does not produce a valid result. Both papers take place consecutively on the same day, with a short interval. Registration automatically includes both.

**Q8: In what order are the papers taken?**

Paper 1 usually comes first, followed by Paper 2, with a short interval of around 5–10 minutes for the system transition and preparation. Follow your Pearson VUE centre's arrangements, as the exact order and schedule may vary. Each paper lasts 75 continuous minutes, with no break within the paper.

**Q9: What mathematics is covered?**

Both papers cover Higher GCSE / AS-level pure mathematics. **Neither mechanics nor statistics is included**, and Further Mathematics is not required. Topics include algebra and functions, coordinate geometry, sequences and series, trigonometry, exponentials and logarithms, differentiation and integration, shapes and transformations. Paper 2 also assesses reasoning and proof within the specification. See the official TMUA Content Specification for details.

### Preparation

**Q10: How difficult is TMUA?**

This guide places TMUA above standard A-Level mathematics but below STEP, and broadly alongside the ESAT mathematics modules. Questions emphasise understanding and flexible application, with particularly demanding logic in Paper 2. Time pressure is a major challenge. Paper 2's reasoning style may be unfamiliar and benefits from focused practice.

**Q11: Are official preparation resources available?**

UAT-UK provides resources at esat-tmua.ac.uk/prepare:

- **TMUA Guide:** format, question types and scoring.
- **Specimen Papers:** sample questions and answers showing the test style.
- **Practice Papers:** complete papers for practice.
- **Past Papers:** previous tests, including papers from the earlier TMUA format.
- **Mathematics Notes:** official mathematics revision notes.
- **Logic and Proof Notes:** supplementary material particularly useful for Paper 2.

Use official resources as the core of your preparation, with particular attention to the Logic and Proof Notes.

**Q12: Can I use a calculator?**

No. Personal and on-screen calculators are prohibited throughout TMUA. All calculations must be done by hand, and the numbers are generally designed for this. Practise fractions, simplifying surds and trigonometric values to build speed and accuracy.

**Q13: How does TMUA relate to STEP and ESAT?**

| Test | Universities using it | Format | Indicative difficulty |
|------|------|------|------|
| STEP | Mainly Cambridge; Warwick and Imperial in some circumstances | Extended written solutions | Highest; approaching or reaching competition level |
| ESAT | Cambridge, Oxford, Imperial, UCL and others | Multiple choice | Above A-Level, below competition level |
| TMUA | Cambridge for selected courses; Oxford, Imperial, LSE, Warwick and others | Multiple choice | Above A-Level, below STEP |

These are separate tests and are not generally interchangeable. Some universities, such as Warwick, may accept TMUA or STEP as alternatives. TMUA and ESAT share the UAT-UK organiser but assess different content for different courses. Cambridge Mathematics uses STEP; TMUA is mainly used for Cambridge Computer Science and Economics.

**Q14: How long should I prepare?**

Allow around **3–6 months** of structured preparation:

- **First 1–2 months:** review each topic in the TMUA Content Specification and address gaps in A-Level Mathematics.
- **Next 1–2 months:** work through specimen and practice papers, especially Paper 2 reasoning.
- **Final 4–6 weeks:** complete timed mocks at 75 minutes per paper and refine time allocation.

Study the Logic and Proof Notes and practise reasoning separately if it is unfamiliar. Build accurate, quick calculation without a calculator.

### Test-day format

**Q15: Is there a break between papers?**

The two papers run consecutively on the same day, with a short interval, usually around 5–10 minutes, for the system transition and preparation. Each paper is 75 continuous minutes without an internal break. Allow around 2.5–3 hours including the interval, and follow your Pearson VUE centre's schedule.

**Q16: What does the test interface look like?**

You take TMUA on a computer at a Pearson VUE centre and select answers with the mouse. The interface provides question navigation, flags for questions to revisit and a timer. No on-screen calculator is provided. Use the erasable board or writing materials supplied by the centre for working.

**Q17: Can I skip questions and return to them later?**

Yes. Use the navigation to skip difficult questions and return if time allows. Answer confident questions first. There is no negative marking, so attempt every question, even if you need to guess. Paper 2 reasoning can take longer, so budget your time carefully.

**Q18: Is rough paper provided?**

Centres usually provide an erasable board or rough paper. Arrangements may differ, so follow the centre's instructions. Working space is particularly useful for TMUA's calculations without a calculator. Select your final answers in the computer interface.

**Q19: What may I bring?**

- **Bring:** valid identification, such as a passport or identity card, matching your registration details.
- **Do not bring into the test:** calculators, phones, smartwatches, other electronic devices, reference books, notes or food. Some centres allow a transparent water bottle.
- **Provided by the centre:** a computer, rough paper or a board, and a pen.

Check the latest official and centre-specific instructions.

### Results

**Q20: How are TMUA results reported?**

Both papers contribute to **one combined score**, reported to one decimal place on a 1.0–9.0 scale. Scoring uses a Rasch item response theory model, with a median around 4.5 and the 90th percentile around 7.0. The two papers are not reported separately. Incorrect answers do not lose marks. Results appear in your UAT-UK account and are sent automatically to your chosen universities.

**Q21: What is a good score?**

The following are indicative benchmarks:

- **7.0+:** excellent, around the top 10%, and competitive.
- **5.0–7.0:** good, roughly the top 30%, described as a common range for offers at leading universities.
- **Around 4.5:** median performance.
- **Below 4.0:** below average and less competitive.

Successful Cambridge Computer Science and Economics applicants often score around 6.0–7.0 or higher. Other universities may be more flexible, particularly where TMUA is recommended rather than required. These are reference points, not fixed admissions thresholds; requirements vary by year, course and university.

**Q22: When are results released?**

For 2027 entry, October results are released on **16 November 2026**, and January results on **8 February 2027**, according to UAT-UK's Key Dates page. View them in your UAT-UK account; they are also sent to your chosen universities.

**Q23: Can I retake the test if my score is disappointing?**

You may take **only one sitting per application cycle**, so you cannot retake in the same cycle. Cambridge or Oxford applicants who sit in October cannot retake in January. A further attempt would usually be in October of the following year, with a gap year, and would count only for the new application cycle. Results from different years are separate and are not combined.

**Q24: Can I cancel or withhold my score?**

No. Results are automatically sent to the universities you apply to that use TMUA. There is no score cancellation or Score Choice option. As only one sitting is permitted per cycle, you cannot choose the best of several scores. Where TMUA is recommended rather than required, the impact of a lower score varies by university.

### Access and support

**Q25: What should candidates in mainland China consider?**

- Check the location and availability of authorised centres; places may be limited.
- Ensure your passport or other identification remains valid on test day.
- Prepare to read mathematics in English, especially the longer reasoning questions in Paper 2.
- Contact your centre early, as registration arrangements may differ from those at UK schools.
- For security reasons, candidates in mainland China, Hong Kong and Macao have restricted dates for 2027 entry: **15 or 16 October 2026**, or **8 January 2027**. No exceptions are offered.
- Follow the latest official registration instructions.

**Q26: How do candidates with special educational needs apply for access arrangements?**

Apply through your UAT-UK account and provide supporting evidence. Arrangements may include extra time, a separate room, a reader or scribe, larger text or assistive equipment. Review can take up to **10 working days**, and approval is required before booking.

For 2027 entry, application deadlines are **14 September 2026 at 18:00 BST** for October and **7 December 2026 at 18:00 GMT** for January. Applying by these dates allows time to book after approval. Check the official policy for the available arrangements and process.

**Q27: Is financial support available?**

UAT-UK offers a bursary covering the full test fee for **eligible UK candidates in financial need**. Apply through your UAT-UK account **before booking and paying**; fees already paid are not refunded under this scheme. Review can take up to **5 working days**.

For 2027 entry, deadlines are **21 September 2026 at 18:00 BST** for October and **14 December 2026 at 18:00 GMT** for January. See the official bursary page for eligibility.
